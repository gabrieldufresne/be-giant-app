**Be Giant App**  
***Strategic Foundation***

***Musings & upfront thinking for the September 17 preview discussion***   
*Prepared by August Strategy / Field Office*

**1\. APP, WHY NOW**

Be Giant launched in March 2025, and by any reasonable measure the first stretch has gone well. The reporting is attracting writers of national calibre, the editorial idea has proven distinctive enough to cut through a crowded market, and an audience has begun to gather around it. On the heels of this momentum, Be Giant has an opportunity to develop an app, and further foundation for a direct relationship with its readers.

* **The industry is rebuilding around ownership.** The most valuable asset in modern media is no longer scale of reach but the strength of the direct line between a publication and reader. The Times, The Atlantic and the Globe are all investing heavily to deepen that line, because the publications that will still matter in ten years are the ones whose audiences come to them regularily.  
    
* **Be Giant gets to build it early rather than retrofit later.** This is an advantage, legacy outlets are spending enormous sums undoing decades of platform dependence and trying to convert habits formed somewhere else. Be Giant is young enough to design for ownership from the start, while the audience is still forming and while habits are still being set.   
    
* **The Canadian context makes the case stronger.** Every publisher in this country is working without Meta distribution, and discovery patterns through search and AI assistants are shifting quickly for everyone. These are the conditions Be Giant is growing in, and they reward exactly one strategy, which is owning the relationship. An outlet that builds a direct channel early compounds an advantage that is expensive to buy later.

**2\. WHAT WE ARE BUILDING**

The app has to carry big ambition and so it is worth restating what leadership set out to do in the first place: *Be Giant is a non-partisan, non-profit media outlet created to uncover and amplify Canada's most ambitious ideas, reporting on the thinkers, builders, changemakers and problem-solvers shaping the country's future with curiosity, optimism and rigorous, fact-based journalism.* 

The name refers to the scale of Canada's potential and the magnitude of what is at stake. 

The app becomes the first product expression of a media entity that intends to matter in Canada for decades, and both the bylines and the editorial architecture already point in this direction.

**IDEA FOR TEAM** Utilize existing brand assets to create ownable aspect to app…

**People, Places, Ideas.**   
Almost every news product navigates by section or by recency, Be Giant could navigate by three categories, which is a distinctive organising principle. Most competitors are working with some version of Top Stories, Latest and Sections, while Be Giant already has a structure that has more of a point of view. Noting we don’t want to remove things that make it functionally appealing.

**Innovative video capability.**   
Be Giant has a Video Producer / a Video Editor \+ an active YouTube channel / TikTok presence. Leaning into this function as a differentiator has a major opportunity to define the app. 

**3\. THE WHITE SPACE**

Most news apps are built around urgency \- breaking alerts, escalating headlines and a bottomless feed of things going wrong. It is an effective model for some titles, and it has left a large and growing group of readers looking for something they can open without bracing themselves.

**Be Giant is already the answer to that. We are solutions-oriented, curious, optimistic, built on the belief that Canada is ready to lead. The editorial proposition is a natural fit for exactly the reader the rest of the category is not serving.**

No one in Canada owns the position of the news app you open to feel like the future is possible. That is our white space, and it should shape everything from the notification tone to the pace of the home feed to whether the app opens on urgency or on possibility.

**4\. LEARNINGS**

We went deep on the products Be Giant's own team named as benchmarks, and on the ones solving the same problem at a comparable scale. The useful learnings are the operating principles underneath…. More?

***The New York Times***  
The Times is the category's gold standard, and the temptation is to copy its surface. The more valuable lesson is in its product logic and experience.

By taking a scroll around the app you can see that The Times concluded that news alone does not create a daily habit. Its answer was a bundle of Games, Cooking, Wirecutter and The Athletic, which generated over six billion game plays in a year and turned casual readers into daily returners. 52% of digital subscribers are now on a multi-product bundle, and bundle subscribers carry meaningfully higher value and lower churn. The Times is explicit that it is not building a super app \- its stated goal is for the news app to be the front door and the launchpad that drives discovery of everything else.

**Takeaways**  
The principle that a news app needs a repeatable ritual beyond the news itself, and that the app should be a front door rather than a container for everything. Be Giant has no games and no cooking vertical, so its ritual has to come from somewhere else. Concepts? … a signature recurring format, video, and notifications tuned tightly enough to feel personal.

***The Atlantic***  
The Atlantic is the closest analogue to Be Giant in editorial character, ideas-led, long-form, optimistic in register, and it offers the single most useful data point we found. App users are significantly more likely to renew than subscribers who do not use the app. App adoption, however, has consistently lagged its other products. The Atlantic also built habit through format diversity, notably by hiring nine newsletter writers, rather than relying on the magazine alone.

**Takeaways**  
The app does not win on reach but on depth. The people who install it become the most loyal, most durable segment of the audience. The hard part is not retention, it is adoption, which means onboarding and the reason-to-install are the highest-leverage design problems in the whole project. For Be Giant, with no subscription to renew, the equivalent value is a returning, mission-aligned audience, which is precisely what proves the outlet matters to its funder.

**What the functional evidence says**  
The behavioural research is unusually clear on what actually drives app retention, and it validates the direction the prototype in development…

- Apps with onboarding messages show 56.25% higher stickiness, measured as daily actives over monthly actives.  
- Push-enabled users show up to 88% higher engagement than users who opt out. The notification permission is the single most valuable conversion event in the entire onboarding flow.  
- One publisher recorded a 60% increase in active users within three months of implementing push properly.  
- A structured 30-day onboarding sequence consistently delivers two to three times better 30-day retention.  
- Blanket sends to the whole list are now the least effective approach. Behavioural and topic-based segmentation is the difference between a notification that gets a return and one that gets a delete.

This is why the onboarding flow in the prototype, which asks for topic selection and then notification permission before the reader reaches the feed, is not deemed friction to be minimised. It is the most valuable ninety seconds in the product.

**5\. OUR POINT OF VIEW**

Pulling it together \+ the position we would take to the team.

* **Depth first, with reach following**  
  In the near term the app earns its keep by deepening the relationship with the readers Be Giant already has rather than by chasing cold installs. As the site grows and awareness builds, the app becomes a natural destination for a widening audience, which is why it should be built to scale from the outset.   
    
* **Judge the first year on habit, then on growth**   
  The most useful early measure is how often a smaller group returns, since that is what proves the product works and protects the team from being held to a number that was never the point at this stage. Download volume becomes the right conversation once the habit loop is proven and the broader audience is there to convert.  
    
* **Habit is what the product is actually for**   
  Almost everything that matters in year one is a habit mechanic, whether that is notifications tuned to declared interests, saved reading that survives across sessions, or a reason to open the app on a quiet Tuesday on the commute home. Our design brief is the return visit.  
    
* **Optimism is the differentiator**  
  The app should feel expansive, considered and possibility-led, since that is both the brand truth and the open position in the category.

**6\. DESIGN PRINCIPALS** 

Four principles, each carrying a brand rationale and a functional consequence. These are what the prototype is built against.

**Built for the believer**  
The app serves the committed reader, not the passer-by. It should reward depth with saving, following and continuity (pick up where you left off function) etc.

**Own the relationship end to end**  
Every design decision should ask whether it strengthens the direct line. Notifications, accounts and saved reading are the infrastructure of ownership.

**Possibility over urgency**  
Pace, tone and hierarchy should feel closer to a considered editorial product than a breaking news wire. 

**Native craft, editorial soul**  
It should feel unmistakably like an app, using platform patterns properly, while reading like Be Giant. Ie. People, Places and Ideas brand structure.

**7\. THE READER**

The audience work already done for the paid campaign identified two groups, and they should carry straight into the app rather than being redefined for it.

* **The Builder** is highly involved and influential in their career, and ready to contribute. Roughly 5.6 million Canadians aged 25 to 54, with a household income between $150,000 and $200,000.  
    
* **The Joiner** sits at mid-level involvement, ascending in their career and poised to chip in. Roughly 2.6 million Canadians aged 18 to 34, with a household income between $100,000 and $125,000.

The useful thing about holding both in view is that they arrive at an app differently, and designing for only one of them would cost Be Giant either its early traction or its growth.

**\*\*\*Builders are the natural first audience.** They already know Be Giant, they are short on time, and what they want from an app is signal without noise, which points to saving, following and notifications that respect their attention rather than compete for it.

Joiners are the reason the app matters over a three-year horizon. They are younger, mobile-first, and considerably more likely to arrive through video than through a homepage, which is the strongest argument we have for treating the **vertical feed as an opportunity to capture attention and own this cohort.**

Because the two groups want different things from the same product, topic selection at onboarding does good work.

Underneath the segments, the jobs are consistent. Readers want to keep up with Canadian ideas without wading through the general news cycle, they want somewhere to put the long piece they cannot read right now, and they want a sense that the country is heading somewhere worth paying attention to. That last one gets discussed least and is by some distance the hardest for a competitor to copy.

**8\. HOW THIS APPLIES TO V1**

V1 needs to ensure that it establishes the habit loop. The recommended baseline covers the native reading experience, People, Places and Ideas browsing with search, push notifications composed in Sanity, reader accounts with saving and follows, **and a home for video.**

POTENTIALLY HOLD on several things the prototype proved are possible (depending on scope considerations/time etc) including deeper personalisation, commenting, offline reading and audio. That restraint is a strategic choice, not an omission. Also, a small audience does not need a feature-dense product but it does need a reason to come back.

The Phase I strategy sessions in the scope are where these decisions can  be discussed properly, with the team and with recommendations to react to rather than a blank page. This document is the starting position for those sessions.

**9\. STARTING POINTS ON KPI**

The set we would watch:

- return frequency among installed users  
- notification opt-in rate at onboarding (\*given it is the strongest single predictor of engagement)  
- saves and follows per active reader  
- completion rates on long-form pieces  
- share of app readers who come back within thirty days

**\*\*\***Downloads still matter, but will matter more as the site grows and the campaign broadens awareness. In the first year they are better understood as the entry point that makes everything above measurable vs the ultimate KPI.
